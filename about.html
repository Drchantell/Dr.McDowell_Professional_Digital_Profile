MY STORY

From the community I came from to the communities I now serve.

I grew up in Brooklyn, New York, in a community where young people were often defined by their circumstances before they had the opportunity to define themselves. I was considered “at-risk” during my childhood, and some of the experiences that shaped my early life later became the foundation for how I would lead, teach, mentor, and serve others.

One of the most meaningful chapters of my professional journey brought me back to the very kind of community that helped shape me. At Brooklyn Public Library’s New Lots Branch in East New York, I worked with teenagers whose experiences often reflected challenges I understood personally. I saw young people balancing school with adult responsibilities, family pressures, limited resources, and the weight of labels placed on them by others.

People are always more than the labels they are given.

Libraries became more than workplaces for me. They became places where I could create the opportunities, encouragement, technology access, mentorship, and sense of possibility that can change the direction of a young person’s life.

My journey eventually took me from youth librarian to library administrator, instructional technology coordinator, makerspace leader, researcher, published author, nonprofit founder, and transformational leader. Education became an important part of that journey, ultimately leading me to earn a doctorate in Organizational and Transformational Leadership while continuing to expand my expertise in technology and innovation.

Today, that same philosophy is reflected in my work with MakeHERspace Incorporated

. I believe access to technology, education, mentorship, creativity, and opportunity should not depend on a young person’s ZIP code, background, income, or the labels someone else has placed upon them.

My story is one of growing through difficult beginnings and using learning, persistence, leadership, technology, and service to create something larger than myself.

I grew through the concrete. Now I build spaces where others can grow, create, lead, and become more than the world imagined for them.
